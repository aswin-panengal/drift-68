# Drift 68 visual system

The user's pinned direction wins the concept roll: original sunlit miniature landscapes, intentionally simple cars, restrained cream/amber UI. The game is an Experience surface. A quiet roadside touring guide informs menu spacing and typographic restraint; the 3D world occupies the full viewport and keeps its own natural color.

Palette: ink #202a28, cream #f7f3e8, amber #e8bb56, secondary text #b9c4b9. Menus use one quiet, solid dark sheet over the scenic still. HUD uses small translucent ink surfaces only where legibility needs them. No neon, glow, decorative gradients, or heavy dashboard.

Self-hosted Outfit (SIL OFL) is the display/body face, with measured tabular numerals. The title pairs a bold cream wordmark with an amber 68 and a solid amber Play button. Desktop puts the controls to the left of the drift, and the tall Explorer layout stacks the title and controls around a portrait crop. Functional SVG icons share 1.7-unit strokes. Buttons have visible focus states, specific labels and comfortable hit targets.

Menus have one primary action, a back path and contextual details. Garage displays one original 3D vehicle at a time. Circuit diagrams derive from the exact simulation centerline. At 240px width the sheet fills the view and the HUD trims optional details; the car remains centered. Motion comes from driving and a small menu fade, with reduced-motion support.

The title artwork captures a drift on Alpine Pass with Close follow and the same car geometry and tire effects used in play. Landscape and portrait WebP crops open with a brief scale transition that respects reduced motion. Title and mode selection leave WebGL unallocated. Craft is measured in rendered desktop/sidebar screenshots, usable controls and dependable lifecycle behavior.
